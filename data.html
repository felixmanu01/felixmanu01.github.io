Heat decides who makes it to the finish.

The Stockholm Marathon starts at 12:00 on a Saturday in late May or early June, right in the warmest hours of the day. London, New York and Paris start early on Sunday mornings. Six editions with confirmed participant numbers show what that start time costs in runners who don't finish.

Research question

How much does the dropout rate rise in hot years compared with cool ones, and how many more runners is that?

  1. What share of starters don't finish, year by year?
  2. How does that share vary with how hot the race was?
  3. How many more runners stop in a hot year than in a cool one, for a field of today's size?

Hypothesis. The dropout rate rises with the temperature on race day. How it could fail. If the hot years don't stand out, or the difference disappears once field size and the 2023 course change are taken into account, the hypothesis doesn't hold, and that is reported as a result in itself.

…

more runners don't finish in a hot edition than in a cool one, for a field of today's size.

How to read the data

Sample
Six editions with published numbers for both starters and finishers. 2020 was cancelled. 2021 was moved to 9 October and is left out, since an October race can't be compared with a race in late spring.
Measure
Share rather than count, since the field almost doubled over the period. A record year can have more dropouts in absolute numbers and still the lowest rate, which is exactly what happens in 2025.
Weather data
Descriptions taken from the organiser's history and from news coverage. They are worded differently from year to year and can't be calculated with. Measured temperatures from SMHI will replace them, which is the next step.
Hot or cool
A year counts as hot when the source explicitly describes it as hot, sunny or a heatwave. I made this classification myself, and it is the weakest link in the analysis.

This shows a correlation, not a cause. Results lists record that a runner dropped out, never why. No dropout is marked as heat related, so it isn't possible to say which individual runners the heat stopped. The course changed before 2023, and the make-up of the field varies between years. The conclusion applies to differences between editions, not to individual runners.

Sources

All retrieved 2026-09-15. The organiser's own figures are the primary source for participant numbers. Same-day news coverage contains preliminary figures, which is why 2024 appears with two different starter counts.

  1. K1Stockholm Marathon, official history Race day, starters, finishers, weather description and winning times for 2019, 2021, 2022 and 2023. Also that the 2021 race was moved to October and that the course changed before 2023.
  2. K2Marathon.se, coverage of the 2018 race Starters and finishers in 2018, that 1,089 runners didn't finish, that seven runners were taken to hospital (five for dehydration), and that over 13 percent dropped out in the hot years 1982 and 2007.
  3. K3Marathon.se, coverage of the 2025 race 19,431 starters and 18,865 finishers, a record for both, and no serious medical cases.
  4. K4SVT Nyheter, from the 2018 race That unusually many runners dropped out, the medical picture on race day, and the comparison between about seven percent in 2018 and over 13 percent in the two worst years.
  5. K5SVT Sport, live report from the 2024 race 18,396 starters, just over 17,000 finishers, around fifteen people taken to hospital by ambulance, and that the race starts at 12:00.
  6. K6SVT Nyheter, on the race's start time 18,300 starters and 17,002 finishers in 2024, that seven to eight percent dropped out, and the organiser's reasons for running the race at midday.
  7. K7Wikipedia, Stockholm Marathon Saturday start unlike most big city marathons, temperatures around 30°C locally in 2018, cancelled in 2020 and moved in 2021. Secondary source, used only as background.
  8. K8SMHI Open Data, meteorological observations Source for measured temperatures. Not part of this version; it is the next step.

Discrepancy in 2018. The source gives 14,640 finishers and also 1,089 dropouts out of 15,755 starters. The two figures differ by 26 runners. This page uses 14,640 finishers.

Discrepancy in 2024. SVT gives 18,396 starters in the live report and 18,300 in the follow-up the day after. This page uses 18,396 starters and 17,002 finishers.

Next step. Measured SMHI temperatures for each race day. That makes it possible to replace the grouping with a scatter plot and calculate how many more percentage points drop out per degree.
